# Excellence Through Questions

AI can be transformative, and it can help you excel at what you already do. It doesn't have to replace who you are. You will likely need to rethink how you do your work. Don't know where to begin? Just start typing.

The biggest mistake you can make with AI is overthinking the beginning. It's best to simply start typing at the AI. Not sure exactly what to type? Simply ask the AI. Not sure how to even phrase the question? Just ask that too: "Where should I begin?" Each question and answer will build upon the last. The results will compound from where you started. Even if you've been using AI daily for the past year, you may have forgotten this simple act of questioning. The question is the basic building block of interacting with an AI to solve any problem. Because that's what any problem statement or feature is. It's a question to the AI.

After you've asked your many questions, you now have many answers. Here is where even more questions become important. How do we know these are the right answers? You need to validate them. Not sure how to validate the answers? Ask. Ask different models and different model providers to expand how the results will be reviewed. Models can differ in their training and therefore in how they answer questions. You can get to better answers this way. Of course, the classic way to validate is to read the answer. I know, right? Looking at the output can be just as important for verifying the results.

In the end, the most important thing for any user of AI is to remain in control of the results. That is, after all, what you started using it for in the first place. To find an answer to a question that you can use (even if that's "How fast can we get an authentication service integration into the application?"). More importantly, an answer to a question that you can take ownership of. Because at the end of the day, the greatest thing we can bring to the AI interaction is human judgement.

Judgement and taste are our greatest tools. We should not offload them to the AI. This doesn't mean every decision needs to be scrutinized. When building a website, you may not care what color the buttons are or what font is used. Let's extend this idea of building a website, say for posting blog pages when you are a programmer with no knowledge of web design. You can ask how the color choices rank against the prevailing understanding of what is good web design in 2026. Think the answer lacked depth or seemed too grounded in the current discussion? Ask it to do research. Ask it to find multiple popular technology blogs and compare them against the research. Ask it what it would change now that it's done all that. The judgement and taste here are in the questions. When you don't have specific knowledge of the subject, you can instead ask the right question.

Now you want to repeat what you've done. Ask how we can repeat this process for another project. Or use the magic word "skill". Skills allow you to tuck away common workflows or even concepts to be retrieved later. The best skills follow progressive disclosure. Progressive disclosure is very much like the theme of this article. It provides the AI with a way to access more and more detail as needed. I have a skill in my personal system on code quality. Within that skill, I have a brief for TypeScript, Rust, and C++. This allows one skill to be used for multiple purposes while limiting what the AI needs to know at any one time. After you've got some skills, just keep asking more questions. How can you improve your AI toolkit?

While you are forging forward unhindered, the one bit of caution I'll leave here is: don't be careless. Whatever you want to call it, `YOLO` mode or `dangerously skip permissions`, it should only be used in controlled environments. Don't know what I mean? Ask. This is also part of retaining judgement. Just giving the AI open season on your information and systems should not be done lightly.

Be inquisitive. That's going to be the way to get the best things out of AI. Be fearless. You won't get anywhere without trying. Make mistakes. Why try to avoid what is going to happen anyway? Retain judgement. The actions the agent performs are done on your behalf and are therefore yours.
