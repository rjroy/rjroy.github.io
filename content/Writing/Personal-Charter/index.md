## Core Belief

Trust is the foundation. I extend it fully from the start. Everyone I work with gets my belief in their competence and good faith. That belief is who I am and I work as I am. It is not currency to be used.

## What I Value

### Trust Given Freely

I don't make people earn my trust. I start with it. When I hire someone, I believe they are capable of far more than what the position is defined as. That belief doesn't depend on what they do. I extend it because that is who I am, not because I demand the same.

### Assume the Possible

We are asked to do the impossible every day, but starting from that point is both unhealthy and unhelpful. We start from assuming the possible. This doesn't mean lying about the reality, it means providing the honest truth. Why is the schedule not enough time? How do you know that? Start from assuming it's possible and do the work to show what the reality is.

### Sustainability Over Heroics

Assuming the possible should not also assume heroics. They may still be needed from time to time, but they need to be understood for short-term gains. These should only be done if the short-term gain is necessary for long-term stability.

The health of the team members must outweigh the cost of failure to meet some defined goal. Even with short-term heroics, there must be recovery. If there is no recovery then the heroics were for naught as those involved will suffer. This is bad for them, the team, and the company.

### Feedback Can Be Necessarily Painful

You are a team member. We are on the same team. I know what hopes and dreams I have for you, and I want to help you reach yours. It is not always pleasant to be shown a weakness. It is not always easy to hear what you thought was your best was not. The feedback I provide is meant to help you grow.

This is a two-way street. I want to hear where I've failed. Especially when that is failing to hear I failed.

### Mistakes Are Learning

When you make a mistake, admit it openly. If you admit them openly then you keep the mistakes small. Discuss the resolution. Others are bound to find the same issues. I expect mistakes. It's how we learn. Make many mistakes, and we will all learn fast.

### We Grow Together

This is the last item in the list, but this is where my work begins. I do firmly believe that my primary role is to help the team grow and meet their goals. This comes from two clichéd, selfishly altruistic thoughts: a leader is raised by their team; and keeping a team member happy keeps the team member.

What matters is making sure that as individual team members grow, so too does the team. I help this by listening and talking about where they want to be and working on how to get there. This is more than just simple mentoring about individual behaviors, but also about making sure there are opportunities to present the qualities needed to show that growth to the company.

## Hiring Philosophy

The job is ever-changing. Today with AI, this has never been more true for software development. AI has not changed what I look for in an individual for the long term. It has only impacted short-term goals.

You can call what I try to do hiring for talent not skill. I try to answer three questions:
- can they learn?
- do they want it?
- how long will it take for them to run?

**Can they learn?** This speaks to the importance of surviving the ever-changing world. It is more than just the ability to pick up technical talent. There is also the need to change as the role changes. Yesterday you are finding exactly how to optimize the algorithm so it's `log n` instead of `n` in complexity. Today you are finding the prompting which will assure that the AI validates the performance of the algorithm consistently as the feature requirements change.

**Do they want it?** You have to want more than just a paycheck. This doesn't mean I'm looking for zealots. Just people who care about helping the team, project, and/or company, and find some joy in the work. They need a spark. Something that will help them assume the possible.

**How long will it take for them to run?** This originally was where I'd look into their actual skills. What they know. I need someone to work on mobile-specific devices, and I would ask questions about mobile experience. AI has changed this, and I honestly don't know how I'd answer this today. How much expertise in a subject is needed changes weekly. Today, November 2026, this at least means an understanding of the fundamentals and architecture. We haven't yet quite gotten AI to take in the big picture. Tomorrow this might be just about an understanding of management, even for junior engineers/prompters.

## Navigating Ambiguity or Pointing at the Fence

We want bold statements to be made by our leadership. Bold statements do not come with an instruction manual. But bold statements also mean bold work and bold direction. I am happy not being a cog in a machine, but it also means that there is a lot of uncertainty in what and why.

We assume the possible of the statement and provide reasonable hypotheses on how to provide it. We communicate openly and honestly. We name the cost and maintain sustainability. We ask questions. If we do all these things then we aren't just assuming the bold statement is possible, we make it possible. This is never easy, but this is the hard work of working on something that matters.

Sometimes the bold statements change. There are a lot of moving parts, and as I've said the world is constantly changing. When there's a new direction, turn. The turn doesn't void the effort that was made in the other direction. We learned from that direction even if the output is not used. When we turn, we pick up the new direction and make new plans.

## What I Actually Do

**I look to the future.** I have a unique advantage from not always being neck deep in the problems at hand. I can zoom out and look beyond today. We look to leadership to make bold statements. I want to make smaller bold statements that fit within those bounds of the team while answering those bold statements of leadership. 

**I listen to the team.** This starts with regular 1:1s with the team. A 1:1 isn't a time for me to tell the team member what I think. It's a time for me to listen and maybe ask questions. Sometimes 1:1s turn into status updates, and maybe that's all that team member needs right now: for me to know what they did. Part of this is listening for the distinction: is this a status update because that's the habit or a status update because of the pride of the work?

**I stay informed.** I'm not just here to work with the team. I'm here to work with leadership and other teams. In order to do that, I need to know what my team is doing. I don't need to know all the details, but I should know enough to be able to speak to the questions with reasonable accuracy. If detail is needed then I need to know how to get the detail with consistency.

**I make the work sustainable.** There is too much work to be done. There is a lot of work to learn from. There is a lot of ambiguity. I help to maintain the balance and navigate the work. This involves asking the right questions, discussing possible answers to the ambiguity when no answers are available, discussing priorities, and understanding team load. Task balance is my responsibility, but is also a team effort.

**I foster team growth.** I've already stated this is my primary role. Growing the team is best for the company. There are a lot of quotes around the need to grow. But I do this because it's what I like. It's where I get the most from this job. I want to see what is the next thing we can do. I want to find how to solve the problems we couldn't before. Growing the team in capability is about understanding what makes each team member them and how that can be shown to shine.

An introvert doesn't need to speak up more. They can learn to speak when they have something to say. This is a way they become the person that everyone listens to because when they speak it matters.

An extrovert doesn't need to speak less. They can learn to find causes to champion. From here they can be the one that everyone goes to to find out what is important.

## The Certainty of Uncertainty

This is a living document. My career path has many turns behind it and before it. How I apply my values changes, but their core does not: trust, learning, and humility. These simple words are what drive the rest of the values. They are also aspirations.

This charter reflects my core. My personal experience alone can change this.
